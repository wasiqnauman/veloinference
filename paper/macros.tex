\newcommand{\HeadlineResults}{Across 6,912 measured requests, all 48 conditions
completed without a request failure or harness exclusion. Above 0.5
requests/s, fixed outer batching formed 1.54--2.90 prompts per backend call
but increased paired p95 latency by 1.05--1.51 seconds relative to
pass-through; the added gateway queue averaged 0.77--0.83 seconds. A
serial-occupancy model predicts group size with 0.86\% mean absolute
percentage error across 24 batched runs, while the adaptive policy provides no
resolved p95 improvement over fixed batching.}
\newcommand{\PrimaryFinding}{At 1.0, 1.5, and 1.8 requests/s, fixed batching
increased paired p95 latency over pass-through by 1,510, 1,047, and 1,370\,ms,
respectively; all three 95\% intervals exclude zero. The policy formed larger
outer calls but did not deliver a consistent backend-time reduction, so
0.77--0.83 seconds of gateway queueing remained exposed to the request.}
\newcommand{\ProxyFinding}{Pass-through minus direct paired p95 effects ranged
from $-451$ to $+251$\,ms, and every 95\% interval crossed zero. This experiment
therefore does not resolve a latency penalty from translation and telemetry
alone.}
\newcommand{\AdaptiveFinding}{Adaptive-minus-fixed paired p95 effects ranged
from $-650$ to $+216$\,ms, with every 95\% interval crossing zero. The frozen
adaptive closure policy did not produce a repeatable tail-latency advantage.}
