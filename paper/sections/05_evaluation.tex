\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The analyzer accepted all 48 declared conditions and excluded none. Across
6,912 measured requests, every request completed successfully. No condition
crossed the harness-invalid threshold; the largest condition-level p95 arrival
drift was 2.280\,ms. We report each scheduled condition, including neutral
effects, rather than selecting rates after observing the results.

\subsection{RQ1: What does the pass-through layer cost?}

\ProxyFinding{} The paired p95 deltas (pass-through minus direct) were
$-180$\,ms [95\% CI: $-676$, 317] at 0.5 requests/s,
$-451$\,ms [$-1{,}662$, 761] at 1.0 requests/s,
$+162$\,ms [$-309$, 634] at 1.5 requests/s, and
$+251$\,ms [$-424$, 925] at 1.8 requests/s. The direction changes with load,
and the intervals are wide relative to the means. These data support using
pass-through as the outer-scheduler baseline, but not the stronger claim that
its overhead is zero.

\subsection{RQ2: Does fixed outer batching help?}

\PrimaryFinding{} At 0.5 requests/s, the fixed policy remained at 1.00 prompt
per backend call and changed paired p95 latency by $-123$\,ms [95\% CI:
$-754$, 508]. This is the predicted low-load regime: the 2-second periodic
gap leaves no companion for a 1\,ms window, no backlog develops, and the
latency effect is unresolved.

The regime changes at 1.0 requests/s. Mean fixed-policy outer group size rises
to 1.54, then to 2.24 and 2.90 at 1.5 and 1.8 requests/s. Yet mean p95 latency
rises from 1,884 to 3,393\,ms at 1.0 requests/s, from 2,172 to 3,220\,ms at
1.5 requests/s, and from 2,120 to 3,490\,ms at 1.8 requests/s, relative to
pass-through. The paired increases are respectively 1,510\,ms [1,330, 1,690],
1,047\,ms [303, 1,791], and 1,370\,ms [484, 2,256]. The means of the run-wise
paired relative increases are 82.6\%, 48.7\%, and 65.1\%, respectively.

Larger outer calls did not produce a consistent backend-time reduction. At
1.0, 1.5, and 1.8 requests/s, mean backend time changed from 1,502, 1,688, and
1,661\,ms in pass-through to 1,578, 1,529, and 1,658\,ms under fixed batching.
Against those changes, fixed batching added mean gateway queue delays of 767,
788, and 833\,ms. Thus the observed tail penalty is not a mysterious cost of
HTTP aggregation: a serial gateway withholds arrivals while its current
backend call runs, and the modest or absent backend saving does not repay that
queue.

The batch-formation data close the mechanism accounting. Across all 24 fixed
and adaptive runs, Eq.~\ref{eq:occupancy-prediction} predicts call-weighted
outer group size with 0.020 requests/call mean absolute error, 0.86\% mean
absolute percentage error, and descriptive $R^2=0.9985$. Individual
measured-to-predicted ratios range from 0.978 to 1.000. Because
$\bar S_{\mathrm{call}}$ is measured in the same run, this agreement is a
consistency diagnostic rather than an independent causal estimate. It is
nevertheless discriminating here: intentional waits are at most 20\,ms while
scheduled gaps are at least 555.6\,ms, whereas backend occupancy is long enough
to account quantitatively for the observed groups.

All 6,912 requests completed, so the count-normalized completion metric equals
offered load in every condition. This rules out request failure as the source
of the latency differences, but it does not demonstrate equal saturation
capacity: the metric uses the configured schedule duration rather than the
wall-clock span from first arrival to last completion.

\subsection{RQ3: Does the frozen adaptive policy help?}

\AdaptiveFinding{} The paired adaptive-minus-fixed p95 deltas were
$+115$\,ms [95\% CI: $-696$, 926],
$-650$\,ms [$-1{,}669$, 369],
$+216$\,ms [$-1{,}157$, 1,589], and
$-282$\,ms [$-995$, 431] as load increased. Adaptive closure reduced mean
outer grouping from 1.54 to 1.32 at 1.0 requests/s and from 2.90 to 2.75 at
1.8 requests/s, but it left grouping nearly unchanged at 1.5 requests/s and
none of the paired latency intervals excluded zero. With three repetitions,
the defensible result is not that adaptive and fixed are equivalent; it is
that this frozen policy did not demonstrate a repeatable advantage.

\subsection{Validity and negative-result accounting}

The terminal audit found 48 complete, zero harness-invalid, and zero failed
conditions. Interrupted directories were archived before resume and do not
enter any aggregate. Every run records the same immutable model revision and
a clean source tree. The first two direct runs precede a harness commit that
added safe resume and explicit invalid-state bookkeeping; a source diff
confirmed that request generation, warmup, open-loop timing, clients, and
summarization were unchanged.

GPU sampling is not complete enough for a matrix-level result. Of 5,726 GPU
monitor records, 738 contain \texttt{FileNotFoundError} after the restart,
leaving fewer than three telemetry-bearing adaptive repetitions at some rates.
The analysis JSON retains available values and their actual $n$, but the paper
makes no comparative utilization, VRAM, power, or thermal claim. Request-level
latency, queue, backend, batch, failure, and drift records are complete for all
48 conditions.
