\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Gateway micro-batching in front of a continuously batching LLM engine is not a
free second optimization. It is a second scheduler with less information and
its own queue. This paper separates two mechanisms that are easy to conflate:
companions found during an intentional micro-window and backlog accumulated
while a serial outer worker awaits the backend. \adip{} turns that distinction
into a falsifiable, request-level experiment on a single consumer-grade GPU.

Across the 48-condition experiment, fixed batching forms larger calls at
1.0--1.8 requests/s but adds 1.05--1.51 seconds of paired p95 latency relative
to pass-through. A measured-occupancy model predicts those groups with 0.86\%
mean absolute percentage error, while queueing adds 0.77--0.83 seconds and
backend time does not fall consistently. Pass-through overhead and the
adaptive policy's advantage remain unresolved under the run-level intervals.

Within this tested scope, the practical result is precise: a larger outer
batch is not evidence of useful batching. When it is created by serial gateway
occupancy, it can be a measurement of withheld scheduling opportunity. The
artifact makes that result auditable and provides a predeclared base for
randomized, bursty, heterogeneous, and larger-model validation.
